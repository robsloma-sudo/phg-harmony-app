# Hospitality branding, drinks-list conventions, screen formats and US legal: evidence base

Read on 2026-09-28 by a research sub-agent of the PHG Menu Designer. 18 sources were read through Firecrawl scrapes.
**Gaps:** no source was read for the visual language of Latin cantinas, dive/sports bars or hotel bars (beyond the
Dandelyan award menu). The FDA Food Code wording comes from Michigan's regulator summary.

## Sources

**[1] Murray, E. B. (2017). "Cocktail Menus for the Modern Age."** *SevenFifty Daily.* https://daily.sevenfifty.com/cocktail-menus-for-the-modern-age/
- **Pouring Ribbons:** drinks plotted on a 2-axis matrix (refreshing ↔ spirituous × adventurous ↔ comforting).
- **Blue Ribbon Federal Grill:** drinks charted by strength against "classic → adventurous". An ingredient-only list "gives one little clue of what to expect."
- **Nitecap:** illustrated menus of 4–6 pages, 2 editions a year, **$2,000–2,500 per edition**. Guests collect them.
- **Ticonderoga Club:** a 28-page journal menu later slimmed to a plain list to simplify service.
- **Polite Provisions:** a 25-page colouring book with about 80 drinks in 13 categories, each illustrated with the right glass, ice and garnish.

**[2] Tonon, R. (2023). "This Is the Menu?"** *PUNCH.* https://punchdrink.com/articles/experimental-cocktail-menus/
- Paper menus are "not disappearing (despite QR codes' best efforts)". Formats are going 3D:
  - Bijou: a Rubik's cube of flavours
  - Rayo: 10 sample bottles as the menu
  - Anthill: a mock medicine box
  - Gold Bar: half the menu printed in UV ink

**[3] Simonson, R. (2015). "The Craft Cocktail Menu Gets Conceptual."** *PUNCH.* https://punchdrink.com/articles/concept-cocktail-menus-pouring-ribbons-nyc-the-walker-inn-la/
- Craft bars turn menus over a few times a year. Narrative concepts (Route 66, one drink per state) replace the seasonal calendar.
- The Walker Inn avoided the words "theme" and "concept" to stay clear of kitsch. Story menus start conversations.

**[4] Gardiner, K. (2016). "How Dandelyan Creates the World's Best Cocktail Menu."** Tales of the Cocktail. https://talesofthecocktail.org/how-dandelyan-creates-worlds-best-cocktail-menu/
Supporting: Difford's Spirited Awards winners list; Secret DC, Allegory 2026.
- The winning hotel-bar menu uses botanical art, **elegant typography** and vivid colour.
- **Structure: 4 narrative sections + Classics + Non-alcoholic.** Research took 18, 10 and 7 months for successive menus.
- "Be honest and authentic… don't forget where you are."
- The Spirited Awards judge both design and content.
- Winners include Death & Co (2010), Artesian (2011), Dead Rabbit (2013, 2015), The Aviary (2014), Dandelyan (2016) and **Allegory (2026, hotel bar, Washington DC)**.

**[5] Morley, M. (2015). "3 Fail-proof Tips for Designing the Perfect Menu."** AIGA Eye on Design, interviewing Armin Vit (UnderConsideration, *Art of the Menu*). https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/the-art-of-the-menus-recipe-for-a-success/
- Vit's three criteria: **(1) easy to digest** (options, order, prices at a glance); **(2) it has to taste right** (the brand sets the mood); **(3) line checks** (easy to update, reprint and survive service).
- **Brewery (Cellarmaker, by Gamut):** a simple grid you can place a flight on, printed on cheap sheets.
- **Large restaurants:** a "single, large-sheet format printed in one colour… perfect layouts and excellent typography."
- **Clichés:** clipboards; the brasserie "dozen fonts, one-colour etching, some curve". **Photo menus "usually look weird."**

**[6] Smuggler's Cove (SF) and Three Dots and a Dash (Chicago): tiki menus, read 2026.** https://www.smugglerscovesf.com/menu · https://www.threedotschicago.com/menus/
- Smuggler's Cove sections are organised by history and geography, with nautical illustrations and **gold ornamental dividers**. A **"⧫ Very Strong"** strength marker is used.
- Its footer carries a nut cross-contact line, a raw-egg notice, a tax note and a drink-responsibly line.
- **Three Dots spirits line:** *"Brand Expression, Country — age, still, raw material, ABV"*. Flights are priced as a set. The **3.5% surcharge** is printed with its purpose and an opt-out.

**[7] Brockenberry, J. (2025). "How To Optimize Your By-the-Glass Wine List."** *SevenFifty Daily.*
- Offer a few wines per style, use a consistent markup and **pour 5 oz as standard** (some pour 6 oz). Flights use smaller pours.
- Put familiar grapes first for trust (Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir, Cabernet).
- **Price ladder** from $9–10 up to $45–50 in gradual steps within each category. Swap 1–2 wines at a time.

**[8] Miller, C. A. (2026). "What Makes a Great Wine List Today?"** *SevenFifty Daily.*
- The shift is from "encyclopedia to edit". Regional organisation is still the norm, but **style-first** lists are growing.
- Bibliotheque (NYC) splits its list into literary "genres" at the front and regions at the back. "A wine list… should feel like an invitation."

**[9] Blake, D. (2017). "How the Best Bars Curate Their Craft Beer Menu."** *Hop Culture.* Plus Cellarmaker's tap list.
- Balance the taps across **style buckets**: Light, Malty, Hoppy, Dark, IBU+ (>55), High ABV (>7.9%). Include gateway beers and cider.
- **Listing convention:** *Name / Style • ABV%*, for example "WC IPA • 6.1% ABV". Strong or sour beers are poured in smaller glasses (9–10 oz).

**[10] Scholz, L. (2019). "How to Build a Profitable No-Alcohol Beverage Program."** *SevenFifty Daily.*
- NoMad's "soft cocktails" section is so well integrated that guests don't notice it is alcohol-free.
- Zero-proof drinks sell for **$6–8** (about $4.50 profit). Price them below cocktails.
- Harvard & Stone marks convertible cocktails with **asterisks**.

**[11] Kohler, T. (2024). "13 QR-Code Usability Guidelines."** Nielsen Norman Group. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/qr-code-guidelines/
- Label what the code does and show a short URL. Deep-link to a **mobile-friendly** menu.
- Print dark on light, **minimum 2×2 cm**, and add 1 cm per 10 cm of scanning distance.
- Guests need about 15 s to scan, so never put a code on a rotating TV slide.

**[12] Extron. "Font Size and Legibility for Videowall Content."** https://www.extron.com/article/videowallfontsize
- Text should span at least 10 arc-minutes, ideally 15–20.
- **1 inch of text height per 15 ft to the farthest viewer.** Font pixel height is about 30–35% larger than its point size.

**[13] University of Kentucky. "Digital Signage Best Practices."** https://uknow.uky.edu/content/digital-signage-best-practices
- Legible distance: 20 pt → 7 ft, 34 → 12, 50 → 18, 66 → 25, 100 → 36.
- Design at 1920×1080 so each screen reads in 7 s or less (the "3×5 rule"). No script fonts, and avoid neon on dark.

**[14] Android Developers (2026). "Typography — TV."** https://developer.android.com/design/ui/tv/guides/styles/typography
- Use larger type and faces with large counters; avoid thin strokes.
- Use a sturdy sans for body and labels, and a display face for headlines only.

**[15] US FDA. "Menu Labeling Rule: General Information Fact Sheet"** (21 CFR 101.11, compliance from 2018). https://www.fda.gov/media/116000/download
- Applies to **chains with 20+ locations**, and **covers alcoholic drinks on drink menus, boards and online menus**.
- Print "2,000 calories a day is used for general nutrition advice, but calorie needs vary" at the bottom of every page.
- Print "Additional nutrition information available upon request" at the bottom of page 1.
- Exempt: specials and temporary items.

**[16] Michigan MDARD. "FOOD CODE Consumer Advisory (3-603.11)."**
- Mark raw or undercooked items (including egg-white cocktails) with an **asterisk**, plus a footnote disclosure.
- Add a **reminder, word for word**, for example: "Consuming raw or undercooked meats, poultry, seafood, shellfish, or eggs may increase your risk of foodborne illness". States vary.

**[17] Greenberg Traurig (2024). "California SB 1524."**
- Under California's junk-fee rules, a mandatory service charge must be shown **with its purpose** on any menu that shows prices.
- It must be "clear and conspicuous": larger or contrasting type, or set off with symbols.

**[18] Fisher Phillips (2026). "California's New Allergen Disclosure Law" (SB 68, from 1 July 2026).**
- Chains with 20+ locations must disclose the **9 FDA major allergens** on all physical and digital menus: milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy and sesame.
- A QR code or link is allowed only with a written alternative. Cross-contact warnings are recommended.

## Rules for a menu designer (branding, lists, formats, legal)

1. Legibility comes before concept. Guests grasp options, order and prices at a glance, and the brand sits on top of that. [5][1]
2. Match format to the room: a single sheet with excellent type for large rooms, bespoke objects for small concept bars. [5][2]
3. Budget for turnover: seasonal menus change 2–4 times a year. [1][3][5]
4. Guide choice with flavour or strength cues (for example "⧫ Very Strong"), not ingredient lists alone. [1][6]
5. Put the concept in section headers and drink names, not in fonts, and avoid kitsch. Award structure: narrative sections + **Classics** + **Zero-Proof**. [3][4]
6. Avoid food photos unless they are excellent; illustration of glassware is the trusted alternative. [5][1]
7. Use one display face plus one text face; the brasserie "dozen fonts" is a cliché. [5][14]
8. Group by-the-glass wine by style, familiar grapes first, show the pour size, and ladder prices within each group. [7]
9. Bottle lists: style, mood or price-cap sections for approachable venues; region for fine dining. [8]
10. **Every beer entry: name, style and ABV** (brewery and city for guest beers), with a consistent separator. [9]
11. Group beers by flavour bucket or light → dark, and show smaller pours for strong or sour beers. [9]
12. Spirits use two lines: "Expression, Origin" then "age · still · raw material · ABV". Price flights as a set. [6]
13. Zero-proof gets its own named section styled like the cocktails, or a symbol on convertible drinks, priced below cocktails. [10][4]
14. QR codes: at least 2×2 cm (2.5–3 cm in dim bars), dark on light, labelled, with a short URL, deep-linked, and the destination kept live. [11]
15. Never put a QR code on a rotating TV slide. [11]
16. TV text: at least 1" of cap height per 15 ft; design at 1920×1080 with a sturdy sans, and no script or hairline strokes. [12][13][14]
17. Chains with 20+ locations: calories next to every standard item, drinks included, plus the two FDA statements. [15]
18. Raw egg or undercooked items: an asterisk, a disclosure and a verbatim Food Code reminder. [16][6]
19. Service charges and auto-gratuity: amount and purpose on every priced surface, set conspicuously. [17][6]
20. Allergens: per-item codes, a printed grid backing up any QR link, and an optional cross-contact line. [18]
21. **Standard footer block:** consumer advisory · allergen/cross-contact · service charge · calorie statements (chains) · tax · drink responsibly. Use only the lines the venue supplies. [6][15–18]
22. One source for all surfaces: legal lines apply to print, QR, web and TV alike. [15][17][18]

| Venue | Typography | Colour / material | Structure | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Craft cocktail lounge / speakeasy | Illustrated, playful, or refined editorial | Colourful light paper, newsprint, letterpress; seasonal editions | Flavour or strength matrix; narrative sections | [1][2][3] |
| Award concept bar / hotel bar | Elegant typography + illustration | Vivid colour, high production | 4 narrative sections + Classics + Non-alcoholic | [4] |
| Tiki | Display lettering, ornament | Dark and gold dividers, nautical spot art | History/geography sections; strength markers; rum flights; raw-egg and nut notices | [6] |
